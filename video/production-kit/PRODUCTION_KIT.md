| | |

| **Title** | Pop Pop Bubble! |
| **Logline** | A panda family takes turns blowing bubbles. Each family member blows a bigger one, and every bubble bursts on a big **POP!** — until Grandpa's giant bubble lifts him into the sky. Everyone pops it together, and Grandpa parachutes down on a small bubble. Hooray! |

### Why it's engineered to work (Baby Shark mechanic mapping)

| Mechanic | Pop Pop Bubble! implementation |
|---|---|
| Pre-tested hook | Popping bubbles is a universally pre-tested toddler pleasure (soap bubbles, bubble wrap, balloons). We set an existing satisfaction loop to music |
| Repetition | One 4-bar chant phrase carries the whole song; only the family member, adjective, and bubble size change per verse |
| Template escalation | Baby → Mommy → Daddy → Grandma → Grandpa; bubbles grow, keys rise (C→C→D→D→E), arrangement layers stack. Kids predict the next verse and feel smart |
| Embodied participation | Every verse has a scaled pop gesture (one-finger poke → giant clap) — the #PopPopChallenge dance |
| Language-agnostic | "Pop" is near-universal onomatopoeia; ~60% of the lyric is the pop chant. Only ~20 real words need translation per language |
| Toddler-legible visuals | Flat pandas, one accessory color each; bubbles are big iridescent circles — the most screen-legible shape there is |
| Loopability | 2:01; outro sparkle resolves to C major and Grandpa's small landing bubble becomes Baby's first bubble → seamless replay |
| Multi-generational cast | The proven family roster puts every viewer's household in the song → co-viewing, parent sharing |
| **Our extra edge** | Baby Shark's dance needed only hands. Ours works with hands **or a $1 bottle of soap bubbles already in every household** — a zero-friction real-world UGC engine |

**Anticipation → release** is the core dopamine beat (same circuit as peekaboo): every verse builds a bubble over an ascending run, breaks the drums down, then detonates a POP. Grandpa's verse deliberately withholds the pop ("WHOA!") — the fake-out is the comedy peak kids wait for on every rewatch.

**Bar 7 — the "bubble grows" run** (eighth notes, straight ascent up the major scale — the melody literally inflates):

"Tee-ny  ti-ny  bub-ble"   C4 D4 E4 F4 G4 A4   ♪♪ ♪♪ ♪♪ (beats 1–3, riser sweep under)

**Bar 8 — the payoff:**

```
Verses 1–4:  "POP!"   C5 (octave above the run's root), half note, ff
Verse 5:     "WHOA!"  B4 gliding up a full octave to B5 over 4 beats (portamento/scoop)
```

### 3.7 Tempo / feel guidance for the producer

- **115 BPM, straight (no swing).** This is toddler bounce-along tempo — the same zone as Baby Shark. Do not rush the run bars; the breakdown before each POP is where the anticipation lives.
- Genre target: bright K-pop-adjacent synth-pop / chiptune-pop. Clean sidechained kick, round sub bass, plucky supersaw/marimba-ish chords, bell/celesta sparkles.
- **The pops are percussion.** Treat every "POP!" as a mixed event: vocal shout + pitched pop SFX (rising pitch per verse: each verse's big pop is pitched one step higher) + sub thump. It must be physically satisfying on phone speakers — check the mix on a phone first.
- Leave 0:00 and 2:01 sonically compatible (both resolve on C, sparkle texture) — the video must loop without a seam.

### 3.8 Vocal casting

- **Lead:** bright, warm female pop vocal (think Pinkfong/K-pop children's-TV energy), comfortable to E5 with a clean belt at C5 and a playful octave glide for "WHOA!". Crisp consonants — the "p" in pop is a feature, keep the plosives.
- **Kids' chorus:** 3–5 real children, ages ~4–8, on every "pop pop pop-pop pop-pop" answer, every "POP!", the countdown, and "Hooray!". Imperfect and enthusiastic beats polished — the chorus is the viewer's proxy.
- Optional character cameos: one low male "Dad-dy" doubling, one elderly-character voice for Grandpa's "WHOA!" — comedy, not croon.
- Record call-and-response as a real dialogue between lead and kids so the timing breathes naturally, then quantize lightly.

## 5. Character Sheets

All characters: flat vector pandas built from circles and rounded rectangles, black-and-white base fur, one signature accessory color each, oversized heads (~40% of body height), dot-and-arc facial features readable at thumbnail size.

### Baby Panda
- **Look:** Smallest of the family (~half Mommy's height). Extra-round head, huge eyes, single hair sprout. Signature: **light-blue bib**.
- **Personality:** Delighted by everything; the audience's stand-in.
- **Animation notes:** Bouncy squash-and-stretch on every beat; toddles rather than walks; falls over safely and giggles after the pop. Gesture: one-finger poke — telegraph it big and slow so 2-year-olds can copy it.

### Mommy Panda
- **Look:** Slender-round, warm posture. Signature: **pink bow** on one ear, visible **eyelashes**.
- **Personality:** Warm and playful — makes popping look elegant.
- **Animation notes:** Smooth sway on the beat, head-tilt smiles. Her bubble physically bounces; time each boing to the chant "pop"s. Gesture: two-finger poke-poke (double-tap rhythm on beats).

### Daddy Panda
- **Look:** Biggest body, barrel chest. Signature: **thick black eyebrows** and a **blue bowtie**.
- **Personality:** Proud show-off whose bubble wobbles out of control.
- **Animation notes:** Exaggerated flexing and strutting; comic contrast between his confidence and the jelly-wobble bubble (offset-phase wobble, ~2 wobbles/beat). Gesture: full clap — big wind-up.

### Grandma Panda
- **Look:** Round and cozy. Signature: **round glasses, gray hair bun, pearl necklace**.
- **Personality:** Secretly the coolest — her bubble has glitter inside.
- **Animation notes:** Wiggle-dance is her signature: hip sway on 8ths, shoulders on quarters. Her bubble carries an internal particle system (stars/sparkles swirling). Gesture: wiggle-then-clap combo.
- **Prop note:** her bubble is the only one with internal contents — it reads as "special" and earns the sparkle arpeggios that run under her whole verse.

### Grandpa Panda
- **Look:** Slightly stooped, thin limbs. Signature: **glasses, big white mustache, wooden cane**.
- **Personality:** Deadpan comedy legend — completely calm while airborne.
- **Animation notes:** Slow shuffle normally; the joke is his stillness during the lift-off (mustache flutters, body serene). Cane dangles from one paw in the sky. In the finale he descends under a small bubble like a parachutist, landing seated with dignity. Gesture: big arms spread wide… then FREEZE ("wait for it!") — the held pose is the meme frame.

### Bubbles (treat as a character)
- Iridescent circle with two white highlight arcs and a subtle rainbow rim. Strict size ladder per verse. Pops are 3-frame events: flash → droplet ring → sparkle fall, always synchronized to the pop SFX to the frame.

## 6. #PopPopChallenge Launch Plan

### The UGC mechanic
Two zero-friction entry points, both filmable by any parent in 15 seconds:
1. **Real-bubble mode:** film your kid popping actual soap bubbles to the track — the chant's pop rhythm turns any bubble-popping clip into a music video.
2. **Dance mode:** the gesture ladder (poke → poke-poke → clap → wiggle-clap → big arms… wait for it! → giant clap ×3) performed to the song; the Grandpa "wait for it!" freeze is the built-in comedy beat that makes clips shareable.

**The differentiator vs Baby Shark:** Baby Shark's challenge needed only hands; ours needs hands **or a $1 bubble bottle that is already in essentially every household on Earth**. Parents already film their kids popping bubbles — the challenge retro-fits a soundtrack and hashtag onto a behavior that exists at massive scale. Zero props to buy, zero skills to learn.

### 3-phase rollout
| Phase | Window | Actions |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Seed** | Weeks 1–2 | Release main video on YouTube. Brief 20–30 family/parenting creators (mix of mega and micro, across 5+ markets) with the track, a gesture tutorial clip, and a bubble bottle kit. Ask for real-bubble clips, not polish — authenticity converts |
| **2 — Hashtag + templates** | Weeks 3–6 | Launch **#PopPopChallenge** with official duet/green-screen templates on TikTok/Reels/Shorts: split-screen "pop along with Baby Panda"; a countdown template ending on the triple POP; a "wait for it!" Grandpa freeze template. Pin the best UGC weekly; publish a gesture-tutorial Short |
| **3 — Localize** | Weeks 6–12 | Roll out dubbed versions (see §7 — ~20 words per language) prioritized by early watch-time geography. Re-seed with local creators per market; the chant travels untouched |

### Platform strategy
- **YouTube (main engine):** full 2:01 video, loop-friendly; 1-hour and 12-hour loop compilations once the single proves retention (loop videos are where kids' watch time compounds). Title/thumbnail A-B tests on bubble size vs character close-up.
- **YouTube Shorts / TikTok / Reels:** one 9:16 cutdown **per verse** (each verse is a self-contained ~17 s unit with its own gesture — five ready-made Shorts), plus the gag + finale as the hero cutdown (~29 s). Every cutdown ends on its POP for a clean loop.
- **Spotify/Apple/Amazon Kids playlists:** audio release simultaneous with video.
- **Owned site:** landing page with the embedded video and character page (Deliverable C in this repo).

### KPIs
| Metric | Target (first 90 days) | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Average % viewed (main video) | >70% | Toddler retention is the algorithm's #1 signal |
| Replays per unique viewer | >3 | Loopability proof; drives autoplay compounding |
| #PopPopChallenge UGC clips | 5,000+ | The UGC flywheel is the growth engine |
| UGC ratio (real-bubble : dance) | tracked, not targeted | Tells us which template to double down on |
| Shorts → main video click-through | >5% | Cutdowns must feed the main asset |
| Non-English watch time share | >40% by day 90 | Validates language-agnostic design & localization order |
| Subscriber conversion per 1k views | benchmark vs kids-music median | Franchise potential (verse 2 of the IP) |

Priority languages driven by Phase-1 watch-time data; Arabic and Spanish are strong first candidates. Each localization is a vocal-only re-record over the same instrumental master.
